The Ninth-Day Rhythm

The market has a rhythm… and it doesn’t read the news.

Mike’s résumé isn’t the one you’d expect from a stock analyst.

Before he was a full-time trader, Mike spent 20 years in the U.S. Air Force, retiring as a lieutenant colonel. He wrote code for the Air Force, worked on a cryptography project with the NSA, and helped install an early version of the internet at the Pentagon.

So when he went looking for the day of the month that tested best… and he didn’t start with a theory.

He started testing. Different entry days and schedules. And one number kept coming back.

The ninth trading day. (Write this down.)

“Most institutional investors, pension funds, hedge funds, mutual funds, make their major allocation decisions at the beginning of each month… But by the ninth trading day, the fog clears. Institutional money has been deployed. Earnings dust settled. And the true momentum leaders separate themselves from all the noise.”

– Mike Carr, Chief Quantitative Strategist @ TradeSmith

That’s Mike’s explanation, and we can’t prove it’s the reason. What we can show is that in TradeSmith’s tests, trading on that day delivered the best balance of risk and reward among what we had examined.

So, here’s the exact process you could go through:

  1. On the ninth trading day of the month, look at every stock in the S&P 500 and how it performed since the ninth trading day of the month before.
  2. Make a list of the best performers.
  3. Narrow down that list to the five strongest tickers. (We used Mike’s algorithm to make this easier.)
  4. Hold them for about a month, then repeat.
